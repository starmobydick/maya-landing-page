Chapter 01

The Hidden Tax

Your phone rings 100 times a week. You answer 62. The other 38 went to your competitor — and you never even saw the bill.

Most HVAC owners track marketing spend tightly. Very few track lead loss. That's the hidden tax.

Industry data on contractor call answer rates is grim. 38% of inbound calls to HVAC and roofing businesses go unanswered or hit voicemail. Of the missed calls, fewer than 8% result in a callback within 24 hours that actually books a job. The rest are gone forever.

Here's what that looks like in real numbers for a mid-market HVAC shop:

  • 150 inbound calls per week
  • 38% miss rate = 57 missed calls per week
  • Of those 57, maybe 30 are genuine service requests (vs. spam, wrong numbers, etc.)
  • Of those 30, 30% would book a service call at ~$350 avg = $3,150
  • Of those 30, 5% would book a repair or install at ~$6,500 avg = $9,750

Weekly lost revenue: ~$12,900. Annual: ~$670,000.

That's not an exaggeration or a worst case. That's what an average busy HVAC owner is leaving on the table every year because the front office cannot physically answer every call.

The good news: the fix isn't hiring more CSRs. It's switching the medium.

Chapter 02

The 5-Second Rule

First responder wins 78% of the time. The race ends in 5 seconds, not 5 minutes.

The Harvard Business Review famously found that companies who respond to a web lead in under 5 minutes are 9x more likely to convert than those who take 10+ minutes. Most HVAC owners have heard this stat.

What they haven't heard: in 2026, that 5-minute window has compressed to 5 seconds — because of SMS.

Modern homeowners search "AC repair near me" on Google, click 3 ads in 30 seconds, and reach out to whichever business responds first. The first to reply wins the lead 78% of the time. The other businesses paid for the click and got nothing.

Why is SMS the only channel that can play this game?

  • Voicemail: average response time 4–6 hours. Listened-to rate: 20%.
  • Web forms: average response time 2–4 hours. Reply rate: ~60%.
  • Live phone answer: possible, but requires staffing 24/7. Cost: $40K+ per CSR per year.
  • SMS (automated): average response time 5–15 seconds. Read rate: 98% within 3 minutes.

The math is obvious. The contractor who replies fastest wins. SMS is the only channel where a small shop can compete head-to-head with a national chain — because automated SMS doesn't care if it's 11 PM on Saturday or 4 AM on Tuesday.

Chapter 03

Why SMS Beats Voicemail

Homeowners under stress don't talk. They type.

Five reasons SMS wins the after-hours race:

1. Open rate

Voicemail: ~20% listened to within 24 hours. SMS: 98% read within 3 minutes. Your customers are looking at their phone every 4 minutes anyway. They're not driving to your office to listen to voicemail.

2. Stress channel

When a basement is flooding or AC died at 95°, homeowners don't want to talk. They want to type a short message and get an answer. SMS is the low-friction channel for high-stress situations — which is exactly when HVAC sells.

3. The thumb is already there

The same hand holding the phone to read your Google ad is one tap away from texting. Voicemail requires: call → wait for tone → leave a coherent message while panicking. SMS requires four words: "AC not cooling, help."

4. Audit trail

Texts are archived. Voicemails get forgotten. When the dispatcher needs to know what the customer originally said, the text thread is right there — full context, no transcription errors.

5. Asynchronous

The customer texts at 11 PM, gets a response at 11:00:05 PM, books a 10 AM slot the next day. No real-time human availability required — which is exactly the constraint you're trying to escape.

Every modern industry already made this move: airlines text flight changes, banks text fraud alerts, retailers text order updates. Home services is years behind. The contractors who switch first eat first.

Chapter 04

The Qualification Flow

Don't qualify out. Qualify in. Let the tech upsell.

The biggest mistake contractors make when they think about SMS booking is they over-qualify. They imagine asking 15 questions about service area, exact AC model, age of the unit, financing preferences, and warranty status — before booking.

This is wrong. Every additional question is a chance for the prospect to drop off. The correct flow is the 5-question maximum:

1. Service type

"Is this for your home or commercial?" Filters out commercial if you only do residential.

2. Problem

"What's going on with your system?" Free-text. Helps the tech prep, but doesn't restrict the booking.

3. Location

"What's your ZIP code?" Filters out-of-service-area before booking the appointment.

4. Urgency

"Is this an emergency or can it wait a day or two?" Routes emergency to the on-call dispatcher; routes non-emergency to the next available slot.

5. Booking offer

"We can have a tech out tomorrow 10–12 or 2–4. Which works?" Two real slots. Customer picks one. Booked.

That's it. Five messages, about 60 seconds, booked appointment.

What you do NOT ask in this flow:

  • Pricing (let the tech see the unit first)
  • Financing (post-quote conversation)
  • AC model / age / serial number (tech can ID on-site)
  • Warranty status (sell that after the visit)

The job of SMS booking is to get the tech to the door. Everything else is upsell. Your techs are dramatically better at upselling in-person than your CSR is over SMS — so don't waste the conversation on it.

Common Concerns

Every objection has a real answer. Here are the ones I get on every call.

"What if Maya gets a question wrong?"

Maya is trained on your specific service offerings, pricing rules, and service area. If she encounters anything she isn't 100% sure about, she escalates to your team via SMS or Slack with full context — so a human always closes the gap. You stay in control.

"Will it sound robotic? My customers will hate that."

Maya speaks the way your best CSR speaks: short, friendly, no jargon. We tune her tone during setup to match your brand voice. Most homeowners can't tell they're texting an AI, and frankly, they don't care — they care that someone answered fast.

"How do I know she's actually replying in 5 seconds?"

Every conversation is logged with timestamps. You can audit response times any day. We send weekly reports showing average response time, booking rate, and escalation rate. If response time ever exceeds 30 seconds, we get a Slack alert and fix it before you notice.

"What about spam texts?"

Maya filters spam automatically (obvious patterns: free crypto, casino, gibberish). For edge cases, she politely doesn't engage. You don't pay for spam interactions.

"Can my customers still call if they want to?"

Yes. The Twilio number takes both calls and texts. Calls forward to your existing line. Maya only handles inbound texts. Customers who want to hear a voice still can.

"What if a customer wants to talk to a human?"

Maya recognizes phrases like "I want to talk to someone" or "can you call me" and escalates immediately to your dispatcher via SMS or Slack. No customer is ever stuck talking to AI when they don't want to.

"What's the pricing?"

Setup fee is one-time. Monthly fee is flat. Volume is unlimited. Exact numbers depend on your shop size and integration complexity — we walk through it on the setup call. No retainer, no per-message fees, no surprise invoices.
