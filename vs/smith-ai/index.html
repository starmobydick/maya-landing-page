The Real Difference

Three ways Maya and Smith.ai serve fundamentally different lead channels.

"Smith.ai answers your phone. Maya answers your texts."

Homeowners increasingly reach out via text — especially for after-hours emergencies. When a pipe bursts at midnight or a roof is leaking during a storm, the homeowner opens their messages app, not a phone dialer. Smith.ai's phone-first model means those leads land in a voicemail box or an after-hours queue. Maya captures them the moment they text, with a response in under 5 seconds.

"Smith.ai charges per minute. Maya charges per booking."

With Smith.ai, you pay for every minute a human agent spends on the phone — whether that caller becomes a job or not. A high-volume HVAC shop can easily spend $400–$600/month in per-minute fees on calls that never convert. Maya's per-booked-appointment model means you only pay when she delivers a calendared job. No risk of paying for tire-kickers, wrong numbers, or existing customers calling to check status.

"Smith.ai is general-purpose. Maya is trade-specific."

A Smith.ai agent is trained to handle calls for law firms, marketing agencies, and dental offices — the intake questions are generic. Maya knows to ask about system age and filter type for HVAC leads, storm event date and insurance status for roofing leads, and fuel type for water heater replacement. Your tech arrives with the right information. The job is sold before they knock on the door.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Smith.ai good for contractors?
Smith.ai is a solid phone receptionist for businesses that get most leads via phone during business hours. For HVAC and roofing contractors who miss calls after 5pm and on weekends — when most emergency texts come in — Maya's SMS-native, 24/7 approach captures leads that Smith.ai misses entirely.
Can I use both Smith.ai and Maya?
Yes. Some contractors use Smith.ai for inbound phone calls and Maya for SMS/text leads. They're complementary channels. If you're choosing one, Maya covers the higher-volume after-hours emergency window at a lower cost-per-booking.
